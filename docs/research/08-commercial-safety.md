# Commercial-safe model and dataset strategy

> Research record for **[Wayfinder Research] Establish a commercial-safe model and dataset strategy** (issue #8), parent map #1. Blocked by #3, now resolved.
>
> Much of this ticket was answered in pieces while the Brain was built — #13 audited DeepDarts' rights, #16 audited the framework licences, #24 audited the ecosystem, and the data-strategy decision acted on all three. What did not exist was the audit **in one place**, and the governance plan #8 explicitly asks for. That is what this adds, along with one live decision nobody has taken.

## 1. Position

**Dart Vision owns everything it ships.** No third-party dataset, no third-party weights, no copyleft framework anywhere near the model.

That is not a cautious posture arrived at gradually. It is the conclusion of three separate audits that each hit the same wall, and it was bought by a real decision: abandoning 16,000 free annotated images because their licence could not be established.

| Layer | Position |
| --- | --- |
| **Training data** | Synthetic, generated by our renderer (#25), plus captures we own (#26) |
| **Model weights** | Trained from scratch by us; published under our terms (#22) |
| **Model code** | Ours, on a `timm` backbone (Apache-2.0) |
| **Framework** | PyTorch (BSD-3). **Never Ultralytics** |
| **Third-party data** | None, at any tier, including the evaluation holdout (#19) |

The full component-by-component audit lives in [`docs/licence-register.md`](../licence-register.md), which is checked by a test.

## 2. The three findings this rests on

Each was expensive to establish, and the reasoning matters more than the conclusion because the same trap recurs.

**#13 — DeepDarts has no licence.** No `LICENSE` file in the repository (all four conventional paths 404 while the rest of the tree fetches), and the dataset's own terms could not be read. The default is all rights reserved. IEEE's site terms close the escape route explicitly: *"Nothing on these sites shall be construed as conferring any license... whether by estoppel, implication or otherwise."* Availability, 55,659 downloads and an `open-access/` URL confer nothing.

**#16 — Ultralytics' AGPL reaches the weights.** This is the subtle one and the most dangerous. The licence covers not only the training code but the models produced by it, so the obvious "just fine-tune YOLO11-pose" path would encumber the published artifact itself — independently of any data question. #16 calls it the single most likely way the project poisons its own Brain, and it would be discovered late, at publication.

**#24 — the whole open dart-CV ecosystem is copyleft, non-commercial or unlicensed.** Not one awkward dataset: the pattern. `dart-sense` is CC BY-NC *and* carries Ultralytics exposure, and its NC licence is plausibly a consequence of harvesting YouTube frames — you cannot license out what you never cleared. The one permissive project found (`lksmlr/autodarts`, Apache-2.0) ships no data and uses the wrong camera topology.

Three independent audits, one answer: **there is nothing here to safely reuse.** Which is why the strategy is to own the corpus rather than to shop for one.

## 3. What can be reused, studied, or must be built

| Component | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **Published papers** — DeepDarts, `dart-sense` | **Reuse the findings.** Reading and citing research needs no licence. #13's methodological conclusions are recorded and used throughout |
| **Board geometry** — BDO dimensions, homography, polar mapping | **Reuse freely.** Standard measurements and mathematics; `geometry/board.py` is clean-room from published dimensions |
| **`timm`, PyTorch, NumPy, Pillow** | **Reuse.** Permissive, checked, registered |
| **`lksmlr/autodarts`** (Apache-2.0) | **Reference for a later multi-camera tier**, if #2's escalation condition fires. Permissive, so reuse would be possible — but not needed now |
| **DeepDarts code, data, weights** | **Study the paper only. Take nothing.** Including from forks and mirrors: a re-uploader cannot grant rights they do not hold |
| **`dart-sense`** | **Study only.** Non-commercial, and doubly encumbered |
| **Ultralytics, in any form** | **Never**, unless someone buys an Enterprise Licence and writes it down |
| **Training data** | **Build.** This is the whole data-strategy decision |

## 4. The one live decision: pretrained backbone weights

Everything above is settled. This is not, and it should be taken deliberately rather than inherited from a default.

`TrainConfig.pretrained` defaults to `False` — the model trains from scratch. That is unambiguously safe and it has a real cost: ImageNet-pretrained initialisation substantially improves sample efficiency, and **this project is short of data by design**. The cost lands exactly where it hurts most.

The complication is the one #16 flagged: *pretrained weights carry terms separate from the code that trained them.* `timm` is Apache-2.0, but that is the licence on the **code**. Individual weight sets in `timm` come from different origins — some trained by the maintainer, some ported from other projects with their own terms — and behind them sits ImageNet, whose own terms are research-oriented.

So this is not "is `timm` permissively licensed" (it is) but "what are the terms on *this specific weight file*, and what did the data behind it permit."

**Recommendation:** keep `pretrained=False` as the default, and treat enabling it as a decision requiring one verification per weight set actually used — the specific `timm` model tag, its recorded source, and its stated terms, written into the register. If the answer for a given backbone is clean, take the sample-efficiency win; if it is unclear, train from scratch and spend the difference on data.

The measurement that decides how much this matters is already scheduled: #25's real-data-budget curve says how much data buys how much accuracy, and pretrained initialisation is worth exactly as much as the data it substitutes for. **Run Arm A–D before spending effort on this.**

## 5. Governance, made mechanical

A governance plan in a document is a plan someone forgets on the afternoon they add a dependency in a hurry. Licence problems are cheapest at that moment and most expensive at publication — #13 is an entire research record about a licence nobody wrote down.

So the plan is enforced the same way #14's holdout discipline is: by a test that fails.

[`docs/licence-register.md`](../licence-register.md) records every component, its licence, and whether anyone has read that licence from its primary source. `tests/test_licences.py` then asserts:

- every distribution in `pyproject.toml` has a register entry — adding a dependency without recording its licence **fails the build**;
- no forbidden package is a dependency, and none is imported anywhere in `src/`;
- the register still names the exclusions that were expensive to establish, so dropping one loses the reason with it;
- the from-scratch default in §4 still matches what the register claims.

Both guards were checked by breaking them deliberately: adding an unregistered dependency fails, and adding `ultralytics` fails twice.

The register's **Status** column follows #13's convention — *Verified* means someone read the primary source and dated it; *Declared* means the licence is the one the project publishes and is widely relied upon, but nobody here has read it from source. Everything is currently **Declared**. That is fine for development and **is not fine for a commercial release**: turning those into Verified is a one-afternoon task for whoever takes the product commercial, and the register is the checklist.

## 6. Data governance

The annotation contract already carries the hook: every `Annotation` records an `Origin`, and the splits refuse to mix synthetic and real within one session precisely so the distinction stays legible. Provenance is therefore not a document — it is a field, on every image, enforced by `build_sim_to_real_split`.

What should follow it:

- **Every capture session records its setup, date and consent position.** The setups are the owner's own board and rooms borrowed from people who know what the photographs are for. Where a third party's board is used, a one-line written permission belongs with the session, because "a friend's garage" is a fine answer until the product is sold.
- **No person should be identifiable in a training image.** The camera points at a board; a frame with someone in it is a frame to delete. This is easier to enforce at capture than to audit later.
- **#19's holdout is never trained on, for the life of the project.** Already mechanical, via `HoldoutLedger`.
- **Corrections uploaded from the app** (#4 §7, #10) are the one route by which third-party imagery could enter the corpus. They must be opt-in per throw, and the consent has to cover training use explicitly — not buried in a privacy policy.

## 7. Attribution plan for #22

What ships with the published model:

- **Ours, stated plainly:** trained from scratch on synthetic data we generated and captures we own. No third-party dataset, no third-party weights.
- **Dependencies** as listed in the register, with their licences.
- **Papers cited as prior art**, with DOIs — DeepDarts' DOI is recorded in #13 for exactly this. Citation is not an admission of use; it is scholarly practice, and the model card should say which findings informed the design and which data was used, because they are different lists.
- **The accuracy number and how it was measured** (#21's gate report, the split digest, the tier). A model card without that is marketing.
- **The ceiling, stated in our own words**, as #14 requires: what the benchmark can and cannot support.

## 8. Bottom line

The commercially safe strategy is not a constraint the project works around. It is the reason the project has an asset: a corpus and a model that are wholly ours, in a field where #24 found that nothing else is. The licensing work forced the data decision, and the data decision is what makes #22 publishable at all.
